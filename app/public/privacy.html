Privacy policy

Last updated 9 November 2026 (link imports added). This is a small, invite-only, open-source hobby project.

Recipe Library keeps your own collection of recipes: you paste a recipe and it becomes a tidy card. This page says what it stores, who can see it, and what leaves the app. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking cookies.

What's stored

  • Your account: your email address, name and profile picture link from Google sign-in, and when you joined.
  • Your recipes: every card you save (title, servings, times, ingredients, method, tags, notes, whether you starred it), the text you pasted or the app read from a link, kept so a card can be re-read later, and the link and photo link of the recipe's source.
  • Invites: for admins, the invite codes you've created.
  • A request for a code: if you ask for an invite code, your email address, name and message, until you join or an admin dismisses it. Only admins see it, so they can email you a code.

The browser also keeps your sign-in session and a pending invite code in its local storage. The database also counts your assistant requests for the current day. Nothing else is stored about you.

Where it's stored

In a Supabase database in Ireland (AWS eu-west-1). Google handles sign-in. The site itself is served by GitHub Pages. An encrypted backup of the database is made weekly and kept for 90 days, readable only by the app's owner.

Who can see it

Your recipes are private: only you can see them. Other members can't. The app's owner can technically access the database to run and repair it, and doesn't read your recipes otherwise.

What leaves the app

  • The assistant: when you press "Make the card", the title you typed and the text you pasted are sent to Groq, which turns them into a card. Only that text is sent, not your other recipes. Groq processes it in the US and doesn't use it to train models. Each member can make 50 assistant requests a day. "Suggest a method" sends the card's title, ingredients and notes the same way.
  • Links: when you paste a link, the app's server fetches that page (a recipe site or a public Instagram post) and sends its text to Groq as above. The site sees a request from the app's server, not from you, and never learns who asked.
  • Recipe photos: a card made from a recipe site can show that site's photo, loaded from the site itself. Your browser then asks that site for the picture, as when you visit it, without saying which page you came from.

Nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

Your choices

  • Download your data: account menu → Your data → Download my data gives one JSON file with everything above, your recipes included.
  • Delete your account: account menu → Your data → Delete my account removes your account and your recipes right away. Copies age out of the backups within 90 days.
  • Accounts that sign in but never redeem an invite are deleted automatically after about a month.

Questions

Open an issue at github.com/slatiner/recipe-library/issues. Issues are public, so don't put personal details in them.
